Record journey · where the vaccination record actually breaks
The record is created once, ignored for a year, then urgently needed by a stranger.
Six stages from the vet's pen to the moment someone demands proof. The failure points below are where the paper system breaks — and each one maps to a design opportunity from the analysis.

01
The vet writes it down
Vaccine type, date, due date, batch number, signature and clinic stamp — handwritten into a passport-size booklet. Sometimes also keyed into clinic software.
02
The booklet goes home
Into a drawer, a document folder, a shelf. It is now the only complete copy of this animal's medical history, and it is a paper object in a household.
03
Nothing happens for a year
Boosters come due on a schedule nobody has internalised. A clinic SMS may arrive. It competes with everything else in the notification tray.
04
Someone demands proof
A kennel before a trip. An airline desk. A new landlord. An insurer at claim time. A new vet at 11pm. Adoption paperwork. Always external, always dated.
05
The search
Turn out the drawer. Call the old clinic. Ask the ex-partner. Squint at handwriting from 2023 to work out whether it says DHPPiL or DHPPi.
06
The cost lands
Re-vaccinate unnecessarily. Get refused at the kennel. Rebook the flight. Have a claim rejected. Or hand an emergency vet an animal with no history.
Break 01
The authoritative copy is created in the one format that cannot be backed up, searched, or duplicated — and the owner is handed sole custody of it for the animal's whole life.

Break 02
No digital trace is created that the owner controls. The clinic's copy, where it exists, stays inside the clinic and covers only that clinic's visits.

Break 03
The reminder arrives with no consequence attached, weeks before anything is at stake. Evidence suggests it is the service booking, not the reminder, that actually drives a vet visit.
O4
Break 04
The record's only real use is triggered by someone else, on a short deadline, in a format they specify — and the owner finds out what is required at the worst possible moment.
O2O4
Break 05
Lost, water-damaged, left in the old flat, held by whoever kept the dog after the split, or sitting in a clinic in a city nobody lives in any more. It also cannot be in two hands at once.
O2O5
Break 06
The animal pays for the record-keeping failure — an unnecessary vaccine, a delayed diagnosis, or a history that restarts at zero every time the household or the vet changes.
O6O7
What this shows
Five of the six breaks happen after the record leaves the clinic — in the owner's custody, where no existing product operates. The clinic stack cannot reach them and the free organiser apps cannot make the record count when it is finally asked for. The design job is not storage. It is custody, continuity and proof.
